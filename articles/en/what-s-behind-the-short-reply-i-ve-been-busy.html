What’s behind the short reply “I’ve been busy”?

You’ve just sent a friendly ping, and the reply pops up: “I’ve been busy”. No emojis, no extra words—just a line that leaves you hanging. You wonder if it’s a polite brush‑off or a genuine excuse. The pause feels longer than the message itself, and you start replaying the conversation in your head. That moment of uncertainty is where the real subtext lives, waiting to be decoded.

Miryx reading · fictional example

“I've been busy”

Low signal · Miryx benchmark: 20–35%

It can mean the person truly has a packed schedule and can’t engage right now. It can mean they are using a vague excuse to create distance without confronting the issue.

A clearer version to send: “I’ve been swamped lately, so I can’t talk right now”

Three moves for your next text

  1. If you want clarity, ask a specific follow‑up question like “When might you have a few minutes?” instead of a generic “Okay?”
  2. When you receive a brief reply, mirror the tone briefly and then add a gentle nudge, e.g., “Got it, let me know when you’re free.”
  3. If you’re the one sending a short answer, add a tiny detail (“I’ve been busy with a project”) to show you’re not shutting the conversation down completely.
